Question 1 — Titration

Original Cambridge-style practice question

Procedure

  1. Fill the burette with FA 1 and record the initial burette reading.
  2. Pipette 25.0 cm³ of FA 2 into a conical flask.
  3. Add a few drops of methyl orange.
  4. Titrate with FA 1, swirling regularly, to the persistent orange endpoint.
  5. Record your final burette reading and calculate the titre.
  6. Complete a rough titration and enough accurate titrations to obtain concordant values (within 0.10 cm³).

Calculation

Use your mean concordant accurate titre and a balanced chemical equation to calculate the concentration of FA 2 in mol dm⁻³. Pay attention to the reaction coefficients. Enter readings to 0.05 cm³ and final calculated concentration to three significant figures.

All exam concentrations are predefined. This is an exam simulator, not a concentration-setting tool. Do not use the rough titre in your mean.

Read the burette meniscus

Read the lowest point of the meniscus. The scale increases downwards; each small division is 0.10 cm³. Estimate halfway between marks to record to 0.05 cm³.
